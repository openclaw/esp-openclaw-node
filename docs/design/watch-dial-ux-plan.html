One OpenClaw.
Three shapes.

Watch, Dial and Tab5 select agents, resume conversations, control devices and show results. The screen and inputs change; the menu, features and ownership rules stay consistent. Tab5 is a five-inch touch panel, not a desk: it adds reach for a finger, not a second product. Where there is a microphone, talking is a live conversation with no Send step; where there is not — the Dial — the same scenes say so plainly and route to text.

The same five destinations

Agents, Conversations, Devices, Activity, Settings. One order on all three devices: five watch rows, one focused Dial entry, five large Tab5 rows. Tab5 also keeps Home, Conversations and Devices in a bottom bar, so Menu is a hub rather than the only door.

System state gets a small strip

Wi-Fi quality, OpenClaw link, battery. A working Wi-Fi connection does not mean the Gateway is online. Tab5 keeps the same strip at the right of its header, and anything that needs a sentence gets one in the screen body too.

Mono stays sparse

Values, state flags and short metadata use mono. Names, transcripts and reading text stay proportional. Compact changes spacing, not font size; reading size is its own setting on every device, and on Tab5 it maps to 32–36 px so an em stays near 3 mm.

Talking is a conversation, not a recording

A live turn shows the words as they are recognised, closes itself when you stop, and streams the reply back. There is no Send and no transcription review in that path. Dictating into a draft still exists for people who want to read their words first, but it is the alternative, not the default.

Partial is never mistaken for final

Words that are still being recognised are dim, dotted and carry their own NOT FINAL YET label; a closed turn is full contrast and labelled FINAL. A reply cut short by barge-in stays in the transcript marked INTERRUPTED and is never quietly completed. None of this depends on colour alone.

No microphone means no microphone

The Dial has a buzzer, no microphone and no speech speaker, so it is never drawn with one. Every voice scene resolves on the Dial to an explanation and a working text route, and its Home action continues the conversation instead of opening a session that cannot exist.

WATCH / 368 × 448 / TOUCH + BOOT

Glance, choose, read, act.

A compact system bar sits above the title. Five 64 px menu rows fit together; Comfortable uses 68 px rows. The lobster gives Home its identity without taking over every screen. Home’s one action is to talk: a tap opens a live conversation, and holding the same control takes a single turn that submits itself the moment you let go.

DIAL / 240 × 240 / TURN + PRESS + TOUCH

The same product, inside the circle.

Identical destinations and actions, with a focused list instead of five touch rows. This hardware has a buzzer but no microphone and no speech speaker, so live conversation is not offered here at all: voice scenes resolve to a short explanation and the text routes beside it, and no Dial screen ever shows a microphone it does not have. Agent selection, conversations and device control work exactly as they do on the other two.

TAB5 / 1280 × 720 / 5-INCH CAPACITIVE TOUCH / LANDSCAPE

Five inches is small. Treat it that way.

Tab5 has more room than a watch and far less than a desk. At a nominal 11.564 pixels per millimetre a comfortable 9 mm target is 104 pixels, so the screen holds about four generous rows, or one readable transcript and one row of actions — and nothing else. This revision therefore drops the standing rail and the simultaneous list, detail and context columns. Every screen is one task: a header that says where you are, one reading zone, one action zone, and four destinations in the bar when you are standing on a destination. The scenes, copy and ownership rules are still exactly the ones the watch and the Dial read.

What changed in this revision is the visual language, not the contract. The surface is warm ink rather than green-black, and it carries one accent — the OpenClaw lobster red — which is allowed to mark only two things: the live thing, and the one action worth taking. Amber means exactly one thing, that something is unfinished: offline, not sent, outcome unknown. There is no third hue, no gradient and no glow anywhere on the panel. Cards, outlines and status chips are gone; what separates things now is a hairline, a rule or plain space. Each fact appears once — the product mark and name identify the device, the crumb names the conversation, the title carries the state, the agent face sits where you change it, and simulated is disclosed on one strip rather than on every turn. Type is sized against the density rather than against a desktop: 40 px titles, 30 px row names, 32–36 px reading text, and no essential label under about 20 px.

Three or four destinations, not six

Home, Conversations, Devices and Menu, in a bar that appears only when you are standing on a destination. Agents, Activity and Settings sit inside Menu, and the selected agent is also a tap target in the header wherever it is relevant. Everything deeper is opened by a row and left by Back, so context moves one way and the trail is always the real parent.

One task, one reading zone, one action zone

No rail, no list beside a detail beside an aside. A screen is a header, a scrollable reading zone with generous rows or a transcript, and a single action row whose primary is unmistakable. Live talk, typing and handwriting are separate full-screen modes rather than controls crammed into a corner — a five-inch panel does not have a corner to spare.

Talking is live, and said once

Voice is a conversation, not a recorder: words appear as they are recognised, the turn closes itself, the reply streams back beside an audio indicator, and there is no Send. A live session states itself exactly once in each place it belongs — the crumb names the agent and the thread, the title is the phase, the face on the right is the identity and the 96 px control that changes it, and the strip above the transcript is the single simulated disclosure. Turn flags then only have to say whether words are final. Text keeps its review step and stays one tap away.

Context travels with the thing it describes

Setup counts its five steps in the header crumb. The lamp’s owner, last reported value and request state sit under the control itself. An agent’s voice, text and microphone facts sit under the agent, and a live session states its own agent and thread above the transcript. Draft, pending, failed and reported stay as distinct states — a submitted request, and a reply already in flight, keep their original agent and thread no matter where you navigate.

  • This geometry is a calculation, not a measurement. The vendor publishes a 5-inch 16:9 panel and a 128 × 80 × 12 mm enclosure; the active-area dimensions above are derived from the diagonal and the aspect ratio. Bezel, panel variant and tolerance will move them.
  • Nothing has been verified on hardware. No device, firmware or measurement was involved. Physical device proof is tracked separately and remains blocked.
  • No input beyond a finger is assumed. Hover, pen precision, multitouch gestures, pressure and physical keys are all out. Touch controller variants may differ in sampling rate, palm rejection and minimum contact area.
  • A browser cannot prove physical size. CSS millimetres are a 96 dpi convention, and device pixel ratio or screenshot resolution say nothing about a real monitor. Use the explorer’s sizing review with a physical ruler or an ID-1 card, and recalibrate after any zoom or display change.
  • Legibility still needs a hardware review. The type sizes above are chosen against the nominal density. At ≈ 293.7 ppi a simulator cannot establish that they read well in the room the device is actually used in.
  • Live conversation is a proposed contract, not a working feature. Nothing in this study or in the explorer opens a microphone, plays audio or reaches a network. The recognised words, the reply and the audio indicator are a fixed script on browser timers. Streaming recognition, endpointing, barge-in and duplex audio all need firmware and transport work, and the latency that makes or breaks this interaction cannot be judged here.
  • The Dial’s silence is a hardware fact, not a layout choice. It has a buzzer and no microphone or speech speaker, so no amount of design makes live talk available on it. Its voice scenes exist only to explain that and hand over to text.

The same journeys on all three devices.

First use
Identify device → protected setup → approve → first useful actionWi-Fi, Gateway approval and capability readiness are separate steps. All three devices work independently.
Resume work
Home → Continue → current conversation → result in ActivityKeep the selected agent and thread. On the Dial, Continue is the only path, because the hardware has no microphone.
Talk to an agent
Home or a conversation → Talk → listening → your words appear → the turn closes itself → reply streams back → your turnWatch and Tab5 only. Tapping opens the session hands-free; holding a hardware control takes one turn and releasing submits it, with no review step. Mute pauses capture, Interrupt takes the turn back mid-reply, End stops every timer. A dropped connection discards the unfinished words and offers Reconnect or text.
Choose an agent
Menu → Agents → permitted agent → choose or create conversationKeep the old route until success. Existing controls, results and calls retain their owner.
Control a device
Devices → target → edit draft → Apply → Waiting → reported resultTouch and rotation share one action contract. Neither changing agent nor navigating away retargets a request.
Tune this device
Settings → Display → Compact / Comfortable or text size → previewSpacing and font size are separate. Devices → This device opens the same local control state.
Understand status
Status strip → Connection / Power → repair the failed layerOn the Dial, use Settings. On Tab5 the strip sits at the right of the header, and any state that needs a sentence also gets one in the screen body. Unknown battery is a dash; USB alone does not establish charging.
Reply in text
Conversations → thread → transcript → Write → keyboard / handwriting / dictation → review the draft → Send → replyThe deliberate route, available on all three devices and the only one with a Send. Opening a thread replaces the list rather than sitting beside it, typing is its own full-screen step, and the draft keeps the agent and thread it was written for even when a send fails and you edit it.
Check a size
Sizing review → align a ruler or ID-1 card → calibrated physical mode → inspect targetsExplorer chrome, not device UX. One ratio drives the ruler, the samples and the device frame; the badge stays UNCALIBRATED until you confirm you measured, and resets when the display scale changes.
